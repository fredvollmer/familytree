# Vollmer–Marsh direct-line research report

Research date: 31 August 2026  
Scope: all direct ancestors of Fredric Muller Vollmer through Henry Richard Vollmer's parents, Charles Frederick Vollmer and Doris Josephine Marsh Vollmer. This includes every documented parental line behind Charles—not only the Vollmer surname—and all documented parental lines behind Doris. Collateral relatives were not developed as people.

## Result in brief

The documentary chain from the existing Henry Richard Vollmer to Charles Frederick Vollmer and Doris Josephine Marsh is secure. Charles's parents are Henry John Joseph Vollmer and Metha Peper. Henry's paternal Vollmer line is securely extended, from his original 1905 marriage certificate, to Frederick Gottlieb Vollmer and Mary Kalwiet of Germany. A separate original 1917 death certificate independently confirms the Frederick/Mary Kalweit parent pair, but no record safely identifies either parent's parents, so the Vollmer and Kalwiet lines presently stop there.

Metha's line now extends beyond her parents. An original 1884 Manhattan birth certificate for an unnamed female at 501 West 51st Street names George Pepper and Annie Stelling; the date, place, parent pair, ages, and later census cluster strongly identify this child as Metha. An original 1883 marriage return for the same address and wife records the husband as Themes Pepper, rather than George, and names his parents Peter Peper and Meta Francke. It gives Anna's full name as Anna Sophie Stelling, her birthplace as Lintig in the Lehe district of Hanover, and her parents as Charles Stelling and Catharine Ahrens. The George/Themes given-name discrepancy is retained explicitly; the identical address and wife make the couple identity strong. No German parish entry was strong enough to extend Peter, Meta, Charles, or Catharine farther.

Doris's ancestry is securely extended through both parents and four grandparental lines:

- Marsh–Lane: Frederick Heath Marsh → Andrew J Marsh → Malachi Marsh and Mary Lane → Stephen Lane and Lois Currier → Joshua Lane and Hannah Tilton → Samuel Lane and Mary James. Newly located official New Hampshire transcripts extend Samuel to Joshua Lane and Bathsheba Robie, extend Mary James to Benjamin James and Susanna, and extend the older Joshua Lane to William Lane and Sarah. The Mary-to-Stephen/Lois step remains moderate-high because it rests on a near-contemporary county biography, an exact New Hampshire birth match, and an 1891 compiled genealogy rather than a surviving parent-naming original record; the newly added generations are high-certainty original-record links.
- Whitney–Cook–Brown: Amelia Whitney → Alvin L Whitney and Betsey Cook. Levi Cook's original 1846 will names his daughter Betsey Whitney, proving Betsey's Cook identity and opening several documented colonial branches. The Cooke line now reaches Josiah Cooke Sr. and Elizabeth Ring. The Hopkins line reaches Mayflower passenger Stephen Hopkins and his first wife Mary, while Deborah's mother is Catherine Whelden, daughter of Gabriel Whelden. The Doane–Knowles branch reaches John and Ann Doane, Richard Knowles and Ruth Bower, and Ruth's parents George and Barbara Bower. The Brown branch still stops with Perez/Peres and Betty Brown. The separate Alvin Whitney → James Whitney proposal remains moderate/provisional.
- McNaughton: Carrie E McNaughton → Peter William McNaughton → William McNaughton and Relief Gibbs.
- Carrie’s maternal line: Mary A McNaughton, whose maiden name and parents have not been documented.

## Proof chain

### Existing Henry Richard Vollmer → Charles and Doris

The 1940 federal census at 230 South Avenue, Van Wert, Ohio, lists Charles Vollmer, 34; wife Doris, 39; son Henry Richard, 5; and a younger daughter. Charles's 1940 draft card gives his full name, 6 February 1906 birth at Freeport, New York, and names wife Doris Josephine Vollmer as contact. Washington death-index entries for Charles and Doris provide the same spouse pairing and the parent names used below. [S01–S04]

Conclusion: **high certainty**.

### Charles Frederick Vollmer → Henry John Joseph Vollmer

Charles appears as the four-year-old son of Henry and Maidie Vollmer in the 1910 census, and remains in Henry's household in the 1925 New York census and the 1930 federal census. Charles's 1972 Washington death index names father Henry Vollmer. [S05–S08]

Conclusion: **high certainty**.

### Henry John Joseph Vollmer → Frederick Gottlieb Vollmer and Mary Kalwiet

The original Kings County marriage certificate dated 5 August 1905 gives the groom's full name as Henry John Joseph Vollmer, residence Freeport, occupation mason, age 27, born Bremen, Germany, with father Frederick Gottlieb Vollmer and mother Mary Kalwiet. Henry's First World War draft card independently supplies his exact birth date, 10 May 1878, and the 1910 census reports German birth and 1882 immigration. [S09–S11]

An original 1917 Queens death certificate for Frederick G. Vollmer, born 5 October 1873 in Germany, names father Frederick Vollmer and gives the mother's maiden name as Mary Kalweit. This collateral record is used only to corroborate the direct ancestors' names and the Kalweit spelling; Frederick G. was not added as a tree person. [S35]

An 1882 arrival on the ship *Main* includes a Henry Vollmer aged about five next to Mary and Louise Vollmer. The age, year, and later census statement fit, but the manifest image/index do not state relationships and the indexed age for Mary is problematic. Treat this as **probable, not proved**, immigration evidence. [S12]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for the parents' names and the Kalweit/Kalwiet surname equivalence; **moderate certainty** for the 1882 passenger identification; no supported extension beyond Frederick Gottlieb and Mary.

### Charles's biological mother: Metha Peper

The original 1905 marriage certificate shows Henry marrying Metha Peper on 5 August 1905, six months before Charles's birth. The bride was 21, born in New York, daughter of George Peper and Anna Stilling. The project owner has confirmed that Metha Peper is Charles's mother. [S09]

The 1910 census calls her Maidie Vollmer, age 26, born New York to German-born parents; the 1915 New York census calls her Meta Vallmer, age 31, wife of Henry and mother in Charles's household. [S05, S32]

An original Manhattan birth certificate dated 14 March 1884 records an unnamed female Pepper, the second child of Annie Pepper (maiden name Stelling), age 21, and George Pepper, age 24, both German-born, at 501 West 51st Street. The exact parent pair, compatible date and place, and later ages make this a strong identification of Metha, although the child was not named on the birth return. [S33]

Charles's 1972 death index names his mother as “Meta Dusselberg.” A distinct woman, Martha/Madie Desselberg, later married Henry: her original 1919 death certificate names father Alexander Disselberg, records residence at Freeport, and identifies husband Henry Vollmer. The 1905 Peper bride and the 1919 Desselberg wife are demonstrably different women. The death-index attribution is therefore classified as an informant error or confusion with Charles's stepmother. [S03, S13]

Conclusion: **Metha Peper is Charles's mother, high certainty**. Her identification with the unnamed 14 March 1884 birth is **moderate-high certainty**, because the original birth return supplies the exact parent pair and a fully compatible place/age cluster but no child's given name.

### Metha Peper → George/Themes Peper and Anna Sophie Stilling

The original 1883 Manhattan marriage return at 501 West 51st Street names the husband as Themes Pepper, age 22 (23 at next birthday), born at Harsefeld in the Province of Hanover, son of Peter Peper and Meta Francke. It names the wife as Anna Sophie Stelling, age 20 (21 at next birthday), born at Lintig in the Lehe district of Hanover, daughter of Charles Stelling and Catharine Ahrens. [S34]

Seven months later, the original birth certificate at the identical address calls the father George Pepper and the mother Annie Pepper, maiden name Stelling. Metha's own 1905 marriage likewise calls her father George Peper and mother Anna Stilling. The shared wife, uncommon maiden name, exact address, ages, German origins, and chronology establish that Themes and George refer to the same husband in this family, although the reason for the alternate given name is not documented. [S09, S33–S34]

Conclusion: **high certainty** that George/Themes Peper and Anna Sophie Stilling are Metha's parents; **high certainty** that Peter Peper, Meta Francke, Charles Stelling, and Catharine Ahrens are her four grandparents as reported on the original parental marriage return. Dates for those four grandparents remain unknown. Searches of the relevant Bremen/Hanover Lutheran collection produced no entry specific enough to extend them safely.

### Doris Josephine Marsh → Frederick Heath Marsh and Carrie E McNaughton

Doris is a daughter in the Frederick/Carrie Marsh household in the 1910 federal census and 1915 New York census. Her 1983 Washington death index names father Frederick Heath Marsh and mother Carey McNaughton. Frederick and Carrie were married at Caledonia, New York, on 31 December 1889. [S04, S14–S16]

Conclusion: **high certainty**.

### Frederick Heath Marsh → Andrew J Marsh and Amelia Whitney

The correct 1870 Pavilion, Genesee County household contains Andrew Marsh, wife Amelia, and son Fred, age two. The 1880 census repeats the family. In 1910, the widowed Andrew is recorded as Frederick's father in Frederick's household. Amelia Whitney, age five, appears in the 1850 Pavilion household of Alvin and Betsey Whitney; a cemetery index independently identifies Frederick's mother as Amelia Whitney. [S14, S17–S20]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for Andrew and Amelia; **moderate certainty** for Amelia's 1894 death year because it is presently secondary.

### Andrew J Marsh → Malachi Marsh and Mary Lane

Andrew, age 21, is in Malachi and Mary's Franklinville household in 1850. The 1855 New York census explicitly labels Andrew as their child. [S21–S22]

Andrew's New York death-index entry reports death at Perry on 13 June 1917. A cemetery memorial links him to Malachi Marsh and Mary Lane Marsh. More importantly, an 1895 Livingston/Wyoming County biographical volume identifies another child of this same couple as a daughter of “Malachi and Mary (Lane) Marsh.” That locally published, near-contemporary statement supplies the maiden name independently of the memorial. Mary's 1860 census entry says she was born in New Hampshire, while New Hampshire's official birth index records Mary Lane born 3 February 1807 at Stewartstown, Coos County; the surviving birth card gives the same exact date but leaves locality and parents blank. [S39, S42–S44, S70]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for Malachi and Mary as Andrew's parents and **high certainty** that her maiden name was Lane. This is no longer a memorial-only identification.

### Mary Lane → Stephen and Lois → Samuel and Mary → the older Lane and James parents

The 1891 *Lane Genealogies* entry for Stephen Lane and wife Lois Currier says that the couple lived at Stewartstown, New Hampshire, and Moscow, New York. It lists daughter Mary born 3 February 1807 and gives her children as Andrew (born 1830), Nancy, Mary, Hannah, George, and Minerva. Mary's exact date, the Stewartstown locality, son Andrew, and later Livingston County cluster jointly identify this entry with Malachi Marsh's wife rather than merely a same-name New Hampshire child. [S71]

Original records independently anchor the surrounding generations. Stephen Lane's 1841 Livingston County probate names widow/executrix Lois Lane and son/executor George Lane. His New Hampshire birth record gives 8 December 1771 at Stratham and names Joshua Lane and Hannah. Stephen married Lois Currier on 5 June 1797. Joshua's 9 February 1748 Stratham birth names Samuel Lane and Mary, and Joshua married Hannah Tilton on 15 November 1769. Finally, Samuel Lane married Mary James on 24 December 1741; that marriage supplies the mother's maiden surname missing from Joshua's birth entry. [S72–S77]

New official images now justify going beyond Samuel and Mary. Samuel's certified Hampton birth transcript records 6 October 1718 and names Joshua Lane and Bathsheba. Joshua's original 24 October 1717 marriage record gives the bride as “Bathshabah Robie,” establishing her maiden surname. Mary James's certified Hampton birth transcript records 3 March 1722 and names Benjamin James and Susanna. The older Joshua Lane's certified birth transcript records 6 June 1696 and names William Lane and Sarah. [S81–S84]

Conclusion: **moderate-high certainty** for Mary → Stephen Lane and Lois Currier because the child link is an exceptionally specific compiled-family match but not a surviving parent-naming original record. **High certainty** for every later parent link through Stephen → Joshua and Hannah → Samuel and Mary; Samuel → Joshua and Bathsheba Robie; Mary James → Benjamin and Susanna; and the older Joshua → William and Sarah. Six direct ancestors were added. The available originals do not identify Susanna's or Sarah's maiden surnames, Bathsheba's parents, or William's parents. Exact searches for the candidate William's 1659 birth and for Bathsheba's parent-naming birth produced no usable record, so the line stops with William/Sarah and Benjamin/Susanna rather than adopting the next compiled-pedigree generation.

### Amelia Whitney → Alvin L Whitney and Betsey Cook

The 1850 Pavilion census identifies Amelia, age five, in Alvin and Betsey Whitney's household. Levi Cook's original will, dated 30 October 1846 and proved 11 July 1848 in Genesee County, explicitly names his daughter “Betsey Whitney.” Alvin Whitney also appears in the probate testimony and had held the will after Levi's death. The will therefore supplies an original, local, relationship-specific link proving that Alvin's wife Betsey was Levi Cook's daughter. The cemetery index and two published genealogies remain useful corroboration for her dates and maiden name. [S19, S23, S36–S38, S47]

The Whitney genealogy identifies Alvin's father as James Whitney and carries a paternal sequence through Cornelius, Matthias, and earlier Whitneys. Its separate entry for James gives birth 27 August 1779 at Killingly, Connecticut, death in March 1817, and wife Betsey Law; a cemetery memorial instead calls James's wife Betsey Evarts. A search of the Connecticut Barbour birth abstracts found no exact James entry, and Ancestry's suggested 1810 census and Oswego probate records proved geographically or demographically unsuitable as proof of this family. The earlier printed pedigree therefore remains a lead rather than a fully adopted lineage. [S37, S45–S46]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for Alvin and Betsey as Amelia's parents and for Betsey as Levi Cook's daughter. Betsey's Cook identity is no longer merely a secondary claim. It remains **moderate/provisional** that Alvin's father was James Whitney of Connecticut. James's wife is not assigned as Alvin's mother because Law versus Evarts is unresolved. Cornelius and the earlier Whitney pedigree were not added.

### Betsey Cook Whitney → Levi Cook and Betsey Brown

Levi Cook married Betsey Brown at New Marlborough, Massachusetts, on 24 July 1794 in the original town register. Their marriage long predates the 1809 birth attributed to Betsey Cook Whitney, and later lineage records identify Levi's wife as Betsey Brown. Combined with Levi's will naming daughter Betsey Whitney, the parent pair is strongly established. [S47–S48, S56]

Levi's original New Marlborough birth entry records 26 February 1774 and names parents Hezekiah and Lydia Cook. Betsey Brown's original New Marlborough birth entry records 7 January 1779 and names parents Perez (indexed as “Frez,” but the image and local records support Perez/Peres) and Betty Brown. [S49–S50]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for Levi Cook as Betsey Whitney's father and for Hezekiah/Lydia as Levi's parents; **moderate-high certainty** for Betsey Brown as Betsey Whitney's mother; **high certainty** that Perez/Peres and Betty Brown were Betsey Brown's parents. Lydia's and Betty's maiden names remain unknown. The member-tree suggestion that Betty was a Church was not adopted.

### Hezekiah Cook → Joshua Cooke and Patience Doane

A scholarly reconstruction in the *New England Historical and Genealogical Register* identifies Hezekiah as a son of Joshua Cooke and Patience Doane, using the family's migration and the 1746 Haddam Neck church admission of Patience. This is a carefully sourced secondary reconstruction rather than a parent-naming birth record, so the link is graded below the original-register links around it. [S55]

Joshua Cooke's original Eastham/Orleans birth entry, dated 4 February 1682/83, names parents Josiah Cooke and Deborah. Joshua's original marriage entry records his marriage to Patience Doane on 7 February 1705/06. Patience's original April 1682 birth entry names Ephraim and Mary Doane. A second original-register index and the published literal transcription identify Ephraim's bride at their 5 February 1667/68 marriage as Mary Knowles; that maiden name is therefore now proved rather than merely a secondary suggestion. [S51–S54, S60–S61]

Deborah Hopkins's original birth entry records 15 June 1648 and names father Giles Hopkins. Her original 1668 marriage to Josiah Cooke and Joshua's parent-naming birth make the sequence Joshua Cooke → Josiah Cooke and Deborah Hopkins secure. The GSMD *Mayflower Families* treatment and a Pilgrim Hall Museum record compilation identify Catherine Whelden as Deborah's mother and preserve the original 1639 Giles Hopkins–Katherne Wheldon marriage entry. [S57–S58, S67]

Conclusion: **moderate-high certainty** for Hezekiah → Joshua Cooke and Patience Doane; **high certainty** for Joshua → Josiah Cooke and Deborah Hopkins, Patience → Ephraim Doane and Mary Knowles, and Deborah → Giles Hopkins and Catherine Whelden.

### Mary/Mercy Knowles → Richard Knowles and Ruth Bower

Charles Thornton Libby's evidence-based reconstruction of the Knowles family identifies Mary/Mercy as a daughter of Richard Knowles and Ruth Bower. It cites an Eastham livestock register using the unusually explicit form “Mary Knowles otherwise now called Ephraim Doane,” which ties the maiden and married identities. Plymouth Colony's original record independently records Richard Knowles and Ruth Bower marrying on 15 August 1639. [S62–S63]

The same scholarly reconstruction identifies Ruth as a daughter of George and Barbara Bower. Later work identifies Barbara as Barbara Smyth/Smith and locates a 1614 English marriage, but the original English parish image was not inspected in this pass; Barbara's maiden name is consequently retained at moderate certainty. Richard Knowles's own parentage remains unsolved in the stronger literature and was not extended from unsourced online pedigrees. [S62, S69]

Conclusion: **moderate-high certainty** for Mary/Mercy → Richard Knowles and Ruth Bower and for Ruth → George and Barbara Bower; **high certainty** for the Richard–Ruth marriage event. Barbara's Smyth spelling is **moderate**, and Richard's parents remain unknown.

### Ephraim Doane → John Doane and Ann

John Doane's original will, dated 18 May 1678 and recorded in 1687, explicitly gives land to “son Ephraim Doane.” A literal probate transcription has been preserved. The Great Migration reconstruction places Ephraim among John and Ann Doane's children; Ann died in 1654, compatible with Ephraim's estimated early-1640s birth, but no parent-naming birth entry survives. [S64–S65]

Conclusion: **high certainty** that John Doane was Ephraim's father; **moderate-high certainty** that John's wife Ann was Ephraim's mother. Ann's maiden name and both parents' English origins remain unresolved.

### Josiah Cooke and Deborah Hopkins: the elder Cooke and Mayflower lines

Josiah Cooke Sr.'s 1673 will names wife Elizabeth and son Josias. Evidence-based Cooke and Hopkins reconstructions identify that son as the Josiah who married Deborah Hopkins in 1668, and identify Elizabeth as Elizabeth Ring, widow of Stephen Deane. This carries Joshua's paternal line one generation farther to Josiah Cooke Sr. and Elizabeth Ring. Their English parentage is not secure enough to extend in this file. [S55, S66]

Pilgrim Hall's compilation quotes Stephen Hopkins's original will naming “my sonn Giles Hopkins,” directly establishing Giles as Stephen's son. The documented Hursley baptism of Giles on 30 January 1607/08 names Stephen and Mary; Mary's maiden name remains uncertain and is not recorded as Kent. The same Pilgrim Hall/GSMD material identifies Giles and Catherine Whelden as Deborah's parents, while the Mayflower DNA research summary identifies Catherine as a daughter of Gabriel Whelden from her 1616/17 Basford baptism. [S67–S68]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for Giles → Stephen Hopkins, for Deborah → Giles and Catherine, and for Josiah Jr. → Josiah Sr.; **moderate-high** for Giles's mother Mary, Josiah Jr.'s mother Elizabeth Ring, and Catherine → Gabriel Whelden. Stephen Hopkins is now accepted as a direct ancestor and Mayflower passenger. Mary's maiden name and Catherine's mother are left blank.

### Carrie E McNaughton → Peter William McNaughton and Mary A

Carrie appears at one month old in Peter and Mary McNaughton's 1870 Caledonia household and again at age ten in 1880. The 1889 marriage record connects Carrie E McNaughton to Fred H Marsh. [S16, S24–S25]

Mary's official statewide death-index image reports death at Caledonia on 17 December 1919 and identifies state certificate 70627, but it contains no maiden name or parents. An exact Newspapers.com obituary-index search for her name, date, and place returned no match, and a relaxed spelling search returned only unrelated localities. [S40]

Her federal widow-pension index card identifies her as Mary A McNaughton, widow of Peter W McNaughton. The higher-resolution image resolves the apparent filing-date conflict: Peter's invalid-pension row is dated 8 January 1890, whereas Mary's widow row appears to read 4 January 1896. Ancestry transcribed the invalid-row date for the record. The widow application/certificate digits remain difficult to read; plausible exact-number searches in Fold3 returned unrelated entries, and no digitized case file was found. The underlying file remains the best high-value target because it may contain marriage proof, identity affidavits, and family statements; NARA supplies complete Civil War pension files through NATF Form 85. [S78]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for the known identity and death. The online search did not justify a new direct ancestor: Mary A's maiden name, birth, and parents remain blank. The project owner has deferred paid/offline record orders for now.

### Peter William McNaughton → William McNaughton and Relief Gibbs

Peter appears in William and Relief McNaughton's household in the 1850 census; the 1855 state census explicitly labels him Relief's son. Peter's original Civil War town-clerk register gives birth 14 October 1834 at Caledonia, father William McNaughton, mother Relief Gibbs, and service as a corporal in the 6th Independent Company, New York State Sharpshooters. [S26–S28]

Relief's 1880 census entry, under the spelling Releaf McNaughton, reports birth in New York, a father born in France, and a mother born in New York. A member-tree claim that her parents were Joseph Gibbs and Lydia Paine was tested against Joseph Gibbs's original 1820 Livonia household. No female in Relief's expected 10–15 age bracket was present. This does not make the relationship impossible, but it materially weakens the unsupported claim. [S41, X03]

A federal pension index card additionally identifies Relief McNaughton as the dependent mother of soldier William W McNaughton, Company H, 8th New York Cavalry. A Fold3 numerical index independently confirms filing 17 October 1868, application 166719, and certificate 136861; this corrects the earlier tentative certificate reading 146166. Exact application/certificate searches did not reveal the full dependent-mother case online. The collateral son was not added to the people file. [S79–S80]

Conclusion: **high certainty** for William and Relief as Peter's parents. The line stops at William and Relief because the online pension indexes do not identify their parents and the underlying case was not digitized in the searched collections; Joseph Gibbs and Lydia Paine were not added. Relief's complete NARA dependent-mother file remains the next evidence-bearing record.

## Conflicting and excluded evidence

1. **Charles's mother:** Metha Peper on Henry's original 1905 marriage certificate versus Meta Dusselberg on Charles's 1972 death index. Martha/Madie Desselberg's original 1919 death certificate proves she was a different later wife, and the project owner confirms Metha Peper as Charles's mother. Status: resolved in favor of Metha Peper; Dusselberg is an informant error or stepmother attribution.
2. **Wrong Henry Vollmer family in Queens:** the 1892 state census household with parents Henry and Sarah and the similarly structured 1900 household are not Henry John Joseph Vollmer. His original marriage certificate names Frederick Gottlieb and Mary Kalwiet. The saved 1892 image is marked excluded evidence. Status: excluded.
3. **Wrong 1880 Wisconsin Henry Vollmer:** age one, born Wisconsin, parents John and Elisabeth. Status: excluded.
4. **Henry's birthplace:** some derivative records suggest New York, but the original marriage certificate says Bremen and the draft/census cluster supports German birth. Status: Bremen, Germany, high confidence.
5. **1882 manifest:** the young Henry fits well, but indexed ages/nationality and the absence of stated relationships prevent proof. Status: probable, medium confidence.
6. **Doris missing from 1900 census:** Carrie reports two children living although Doris was reportedly born 5 May 1900 and should have been present on 1 June. Status: unresolved census omission or date discrepancy; later records consistently support 5 May 1900.
7. **Peter McNaughton birth year:** original military register says 14 October 1834; cemetery index says 1835. Status: prefer 1834, high confidence.
8. **Frederick Marsh exact dates:** 1900 census supports December 1867; the 12 December date is secondary. A 24 August 1949 obituary index reports death at Rochester at age 81; the precise 22 August date is secondary/derivative. Status: month/year high, exact dates moderate.
9. **Mary A McNaughton:** census ages imply births from about 1841 to 1846, while a cemetery index gives 1843. The official index now fixes her death at 17 December 1919 but supplies no parents. Status: exact birth and maiden name unresolved.
10. **Amelia Whitney death and Betsey Cook details:** Amelia's death remains supported by a cemetery index. Betsey's Cook identity is now proved by her father Levi Cook's original will; her exact birth and death dates remain secondary. Status: identity high, dates moderate.
11. **George versus Themes Peper:** Metha's marriage and the 1884 birth certificate call her father George; the 1883 marriage return calls the husband Themes. The identical 501 West 51st Street address, wife Anna/Annie Stelling, ages, birthplace region, and chronology make the couple identity strong. Status: same man, with both given names preserved; reason for the variation unresolved.
12. **Metha's 1884 birth:** the civil return names no child, only an unnamed female. The exact parent pair and later age/place evidence make the identification strong but not absolute. Status: moderate-high.
13. **Tempting George/Anna Peper household:** a separate 1900 Manhattan household headed by George Peper (born February 1851) and Anna (born April 1854) lists all four of its living children and does not include Metha; its daughter Anna is independently linked to the Harms surname. Status: excluded as Metha's family despite the matching parent first names.
14. **Mary Marsh as Mary Lane:** formerly a memorial-only lead, now supported by an 1895 county biography explicitly calling another child a daughter of Malachi and Mary (Lane) Marsh. The exact-date Stewartstown birth and the Lane genealogy's matching son Andrew complete the identity cluster. Status: resolved in favor of Mary Lane, high certainty; her parent link to Stephen Lane and Lois Currier remains moderate-high because it is compiled rather than original.
15. **Relief Gibbs's proposed parents:** member trees name Joseph Gibbs and Lydia Paine, but no original record was found linking Relief to them and Joseph's 1820 household lacks a girl of Relief's age. Status: rejected as an adopted parent link; retain only as a research lead.
16. **Alvin Whitney's mother:** the published Whitney genealogy names James Whitney's wife as Betsey Law; a cemetery memorial names Betsey Evarts. Neither is yet tied to Alvin by an original birth, guardianship, probate, or family-register record. Status: James is a moderate-certainty father; Alvin's mother remains unassigned.
17. **Ancestry suggestions for James Whitney:** an 1810 Addison household has no male in James's expected 26–44 bracket, and an indexed Oswego probate is not connected to Alvin or Granville by its image/index. Status: not used as proof.
18. **Wrong Betsey Cook baptism:** a Worcester church register records the 28 January 1810 baptism of a Betsey in Stephen R Cook's household. Levi Cook's will directly names the target Betsey Whitney as his daughter, and the target family is tied to Pavilion/New Marlborough rather than this Worcester household. Status: excluded as a same-name person. [X04]
19. **Betty Brown's maiden name:** member trees call Perez Brown's wife Elizabeth “Betty” Church, but no original marriage or parent-link record was found. Status: Church not imported.
20. **Mayflower extension:** the initial GSMD bridge compilation alone was insufficient. Follow-up located Pilgrim Hall's record compilation quoting Stephen Hopkins's original will naming “my sonn Giles Hopkins,” together with the Hursley baptism evidence and the GSMD family treatment. Status: resolved; Stephen accepted as Giles's father. [S59, S67–S68]
21. **Mary Hopkins's maiden name:** some sources render Giles's mother as Mary Kent, usually with a question mark. The viewed evidence proves the given name Mary but not Kent. Status: maiden name left blank.
22. **Barbara Bower's maiden name:** later scholarly work gives Smyth/Smith and an English marriage, but the original parish image was not inspected. Status: Smyth retained as a moderate-certainty alternate, not treated as an original-record fact.
23. **Mary A McNaughton's pension filing date:** Ancestry transcribes 8 January 1890, which is the date on Peter's invalid-pension row. The widow row in the higher-resolution image appears to read 4 January 1896, after Peter's January 1895 death. Status: treat 4 January 1896 as the provisional widow filing date and preserve Ancestry's 1890 transcription as a row-assignment error; the full pension file should settle the difficult application/certificate digits.
24. **Perez/Peres Brown leads:** a compiled *Wilson Family* entry proposes a 1752 Canterbury, Connecticut, birth and father Phineas, and a Massachusetts militia card records a Peres Brown in 1779. Neither record links its man to Betty or daughter Betsey at New Marlborough. Status: tested but not imported; see X05–X06.
25. **Lane pedigree beyond the new originals:** the official records prove Samuel Lane's parents as Joshua and Bathsheba, Bathsheba's Robie surname, Mary James's parents as Benjamin and Susanna, and Joshua's parents as William and Sarah. Exact searches did not prove Bathsheba's parents, William's candidate 1659 birth, Sarah Webster, or either James parent's ancestry. Status: six people imported; all earlier candidate generations withheld.
26. **Current German, Whitney, and Brown checks:** exact and parent-pair searches in the German Lutheran collection produced no target entry; the Granville/Wells/early-New-York Whitney search produced no original parent link and only the already-excluded Oswego probate; New Marlborough Brown records added no parent or maiden-name evidence. Status: no extension in these branches; negatives preserved in the source catalog.
